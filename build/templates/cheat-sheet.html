Cheat Sheet · Standards & Policy
Part 1
ISO 27001 · NIST CSF · PCI DSS
Three ways a bank proves it's secure. They overlap — the trick is what each one is for.
ISO 27001
The security system
Like a kitchen hygiene certificate.
Proves you run a proper, audited security system — not that you can't be hacked. Broad & certifiable.
Tell"Are we certified?"
NIST CSF
The organising map
Like a five-step safety checklist.
Organises security into five functions: Identify · Protect · Detect · Respond · Recover. Broad & voluntary.
Tell"Which of the 5 functions?"
PCI DSS
The card-data rules
Like the rules for handling cash.
Strict, specific rules just for payment-card data. Narrow & mandatory if you take cards.
Tell"Does it touch card data?"
ISO = the system · NIST = the map · PCI = the card rules
Most good controls (MFA, encryption, logging) satisfy all three at once.
Part 2
Policy · Standard · Procedure
Three layers that turn an intention into something people actually do — principle, made measurable, made doable.
Policy
The why
Like a constitution.
The high-level rule — what must be true, and why. Short, stable, approved at the top.
"Access to customer data must be restricted to authorised staff."
TellA principle, no numbers
Standard
The spec
Like a building code.
The measurable rule that makes a policy enforceable — and checkable.
"Passwords must be ≥14 characters, MFA on, no reuse."
TellA measurable number or spec
Procedure
The how
Like a recipe.
The exact step-by-step for the person doing the task. Anyone following it gets the same result.
"1) disable the account 2) revoke VPN 3) collect devices."
TellNumbered steps
Policy = why · Standard = spec · Procedure = how
⚠
The gap: a policy or standard with no procedure that anyone actually follows — the unlocked back door. Always ask: "is there a procedure, and does anyone do it?"
